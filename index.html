Programs

Three programs, each with real levels — not one big class.

Every program is being designed level by level so students are placed where they are, not by age alone. Each description below is the plan for our opening year.

Quran program

Ages 5 to adult — weekday evenings planned

From first letters to fluent recitation, and onward to memorization for students ready for it. Progress is tracked per student in a simple record book parents can read.

  • Qaida foundations: letters, sounds, and joining
  • Nazirah: fluent reading with correct tajweed
  • Hifz track: structured memorization with daily review schedules
  • Adult beginners' circle: it is never too late to start

Opening with the academy's first term

Arabic language

Youth and adult sections planned

Reading, grammar, and vocabulary taught toward one goal: understanding what you recite and hear. Levels from alphabet to reading simple classical texts.

Curriculum in development

Sunday weekend school

Ages 5–14 — Sunday mornings planned

Islamic studies for children: belief, worship, seerah, and character — taught by age group, with take-home sheets so families can continue the conversation.

Our approach

What we promise parents and students.

An academy is judged by its teaching, not its brochure. These are the standards we are building the academy around, written down now so families can hold us to them when doors open.

Teachers will be qualified in what they teach — ijazah or formal study for Quran instructors, and teaching experience for children's classes. We will publish our teacher standards in full before the first term, and every adult working with children will be background-checked.

Small classes, real placement
Students are assessed kindly and placed by level. No child sits lost in the back of a class that moved on without them.
Parents see progress
Each student keeps a progress record — surahs, levels, attendance — shared with parents every term, not once a year.
Adab before ambition
Manners toward the Book, the teacher, and each other come first. A gentle classroom is not a slow one.
